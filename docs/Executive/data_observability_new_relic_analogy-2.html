Data Observability: the New Relic analogy — and where it breaks

New Relic answers “is my service healthy?” Data observability answers “is my data healthy?” Those are different questions, because a service can be 100% healthy while producing 100% wrong data. Every batch job that exits with code 0 while silently dropping half its rows shows green in New Relic.

The problem statement

The hidden assumption behind the analogy: “if the pipelines are running, the data is fine.” That assumption is false. Toggle the two lenses to see how the question — and the failure mode — changes.

The question

“My code is executing — is it executing correctly?” Fast, error-free, available.

The failure mode

Visible and self-announcing. 500s, latency spikes, crashes. The failing thing emits the signal itself.

First detector

The monitoring system — within seconds. Users see an error and retry.

Unit of damage

A failed request. Bounded, retryable, visible in the error budget.

The question

“My jobs all succeeded — is the data they produced actually trustworthy?” Fresh, complete, valid, contract-compliant.

The failure mode

Silent. The job succeeds, the dashboard renders, the model scores — but the numbers are wrong, stale, or incomplete. Nothing throws.

First detector

A human downstream — an analyst, a customer, a regulator — often hours or days later. MTTR today exceeds 4+ hours of manually tracing DAGs and logs.

Unit of damage

A wrong decision. Made confidently, on bad numbers, in the window before anyone noticed. Not retryable.

Today, data engineers spend >30% of their time debugging stale or incorrect outputs, and the first alarm for bad data is usually a person, not a system.

The same incident, two lenses

A real pattern: after a routine deploy, an upstream producer starts passing null in a field the risk model depends on. Every job “succeeds,” volume looks normal, latency is great. Switch lenses to see why APM misses it — then walk the 5 Whys.

Walk the 5 Whys

Each “why” is a hop no APM tool can make — because none of them is about compute.

Root cause of the root cause: nothing in the stack was measuring the data itself — only the compute. New Relic would have caught none of the five. That gap is the platform.

The metric mapping

APM has golden signals: latency, traffic, errors, saturation. Data has five pillars: freshness, volume, quality, schema/contracts, lineage. Click any row to see the translation, a concrete example, and the metric with its threshold.

Where the analogy breaks — and why that’s the pitch

Three breaks matter. Each one maps to a platform capability leadership is funding. Click to expand.

New Relic tells you the deploy broke prod after it broke prod. Because we hold the lineage graph, we can run impact analysis at PR time: a developer changes a column type, and before merge the CI check walks the graph and reports — “this breaks 14 downstream tables, 3 Tier-1 dashboards, and the fraud model.”

There is no APM equivalent, because services don’t know their consumers the way a lineage graph does. This is the build-control gate: the cheapest incident is the one that never ships.

📐 Metrics: % of breaking changes caught pre-merge · incidents prevented per quarter · CI impact-check latency (< 2 min added to the PR pipeline)

When a service is down, users see an error and retry. When data is bad, nobody sees an error — the CFO sees a wrong number and acts on it. So the platform collects evidence asynchronously, out-of-band (never blocking the pipeline) and trips a circuit breaker on the data path: failing data diverts to quarantine, while the Certified View pins every consumer to the last-known-good version.

The APM analogy is a circuit breaker in a service mesh — but ours protects decisions, not requests.

📐 Metrics: incidents where bad data reached a Tier-1 consumer (target → 0) · minutes bad data was live in a certified surface · quarantine catch rate

A stale Gold table has no stack trace — the root cause is four hops upstream, in another team’s domain. RCA Copilot traverses the lineage graph plus the evidence timeline and answers deterministically: “Gold is stale because Silver volume dropped 50% at 02:14, because upstream producer X deployed schema change Y.”

This is what turns a 4-hour manual trace through Airflow DAGs and Spark logs into a 2-minute explanation.

📐 Metrics: MTTD < 15 min after expected completion · 70%+ of incidents auto-explained in < 2 min · MTTR from 4+ hours → minutes

Cost of inaction — in the currency leadership already understands

Why you bought New Relic

Unmonitored services cost you outages measured in minutes of downtime. The damage is visible, bounded, and stops when the service recovers.

What unmonitored data costs

Wrong decisions measured in days of undetected wrongness: >30% of engineering time on debugging, plus every decision made on bad numbers in the window before a human noticed. The damage compounds silently and doesn’t stop when the job “recovers.”

⚠️ Set expectations for the first 30 days: the metrics will look worse, not better. The platform will surface freshness and quality violations that were always there but invisible. Expect a period of “the platform is showing us how bad things actually are” before it becomes “the platform is helping us get better faster.” That’s the platform working.
