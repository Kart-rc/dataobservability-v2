Data Observability Platform · Tech Leadership Deep Dive

Signal Factory — Execution Deep Dive

One platform that answers four questions no single tool can: what broke, why, who is affected, and how it stops recurring. Built as four horizontal foundations by three mission teams of ten — each team owns exactly one mission, every mission converges on one pilot use case, and every milestone is a demo on that use case. This is one program with one definition of done, not three projects sharing a name.

The problem

Data incidents cost ~$2M/yr per 50 affected engineers; cross-team failures take 4+ hr war rooms; silent failures go unfound entirely.

The vision

Every quality, lineage, and pipeline signal joined in one evidence stream, experienced through one product: lineage with health, cause, blast radius, trust.

The plan

Four foundations, one pilot thread, six months to use case 1 live end-to-end — then use cases onboard on a paved road.

The org

3 mission teams × 10 — Onboarding & Experience, Data Quality (sync + async), Lineage & Confidence. One foundation each; zero context-switching.

Your control

Kill criteria at month 6: no real saves and no detection improvement → the program stops and the teams redeploy. Stated up front.

1 · The problem statement

A payments service adds a new status value. Every team's own monitoring stays green — the failure lives in the seams between systems, and nobody owns the seams. Same incident, run both ways:

Today ≈ 2 days to truth
Day 1Upstream change silently maps new values to NULL. Batch job exits 0 — "success" — writing 8% fewer valid rows. No alert fires anywhere.
Day 2A finance analyst notices revenue looks low. Decisions have already been made on the wrong number.
+4–6 hrsWar room: four teams paged, each tool truthfully reports its slice healthy. Root cause found by hand-diffing row counts.
Cost: 4+ hrs × 5 engineers, a decision made on bad data, four teams paged for one team's change — ~$2M/yr in lost capacity per 50 affected engineers.
Target state ≈ 30 minutes
+2 minStreaming monitor spots the new value and a NULL-rate spike at the topic — one hop from the cause.
+20 minOne incident, not nine alerts: "Deploy a7b2c3 introduced an unmapped value; 6 downstream tables affected." One team paged, cause attached.
+30 minCertification gate holds the bad batch; consumers keep reading last-certified data. Finance told before the number is used.
The difference: detection before consumption, one page instead of four, cause and blast radius named — bad data never reaches a decision.

The gap is structural, not tooling: signals exist everywhere but are islands that cannot reference each other. No individual tool can close it — the seams need a platform that joins.

3 · Strategy — horizontal foundations, proven vertically

Foundations are built once and inherited by every use case; but they are proven on one pilot thread within six months — payments-svc → orders topic → enrichment job → gold.orders_enr → finance report. Checks shift left over time: learned at rest first (cheapest), extended to topic baselines, reaching service emission last.

1

Every milestone is a demo on the pilot thread — "first catch," "blast radius on click," "use case 1 live" — never a percent-complete. A milestone you cannot demo is a status, not a milestone.

2

Every team's definition of done includes landing in the shared evidence stream and naming registry. This one clause is what prevents three working islands — it binds all three missions to one platform before their first outputs land.

3

Existing batch DQ is improved and federated, never replaced. The first evidence source at M1 — the fastest visible win, and it recruits the teams who built it.

Alternatives considered and rejected: tool-per-team (more islands — the problem restated as a plan) · big-bang platform (18 months dark, no kill switch, demo risk concentrated at the end) · use-case one-offs (each costs ~6 weeks forever; nothing compounds). The foundation approach is the only one where cost per use case falls with each onboarding.

6 · Execution plan — three missions, thirty engineers

Each team owns one foundation end-to-end — mission, build, run, and its milestones. Nobody is split across projects; convergence is engineered, not hoped for: one pilot thread, one evidence stream in every definition of done, and a weekly joined demo on the thread from week 4.

Lineage & Confidence10 ENGINEERS · owns the platform core
Mission: every signal joins under one identity, and every lineage edge is trustworthy — capture → join → score → simulate.
Squads: platform core — evidence stream + naming registry (3) · lineage collectors — skeleton, emitters, runtime (4) · join, provenance & confidence (3). P0–P1: evidence stream + registry live (M1); capture edges end-to-end on the pilot. P2: the join — lineage × quality evidence × deploy events (M4, M5); provenance tags on every edge. P3–P4: confidence scoring (declared vs observed) GA; UC2 edges; impact simulation + PR-time lineage diff; column-level where incidents demand. 8 · Governance — how leadership keeps believing

Saves ledger

Opens at M1. Every catch logged with its counterfactual: who would have consumed the bad data, what the last comparable incident cost.

Monthly scorecard

Fixed cadence so silence is never read as stall: time-to-detect, time-to-root-cause, teams paged per incident, saves banked, foundations joined (0→4), dependency health.

Demo cadence

Reviews are live replays on the pilot thread — the war room, then the causal chain. Milestones are demos; there is no "percent complete."

Sponsor-owned KPI

The pilot thread is chosen so its health maps to one executive sponsor's own metric — one leader personally invested in the M5 replay.

Kill criteria — stated up front

If by the M5 checkpoint (month 6) the pilot thread has not logged real saves and cut time-to-detect below the agreed threshold — with all four foundations live and joined on use case 1 — the program stops and thirty engineers redeploy to their domains. Offering leadership the kill switch is what makes the vision credible rather than faith-based. Baselines are measured in Phase 0; the gate is decided on the scorecard, not on architecture progress.

9 · One platform, not three projects

The test for "stretched thin" is simple: can each team state its one mission, and does every mission land in the same place? Here, yes — three missions, one evidence stream, one pilot thread, one product experience, one scorecard. The seams between the teams are exactly the seams the platform exists to close, and the convergence rules close them the same way: everything joins, or it doesn't count.

MONTH 6All four foundations live and joined on use case 1 — the first target-state experience on real data: lineage with health, cause on the incident card, reactive blast radius. Go/no-go gate.
MONTH 11Full platform live — enforcement and gating on, edges confidence-scored, multiple use cases onboarded on the paved road.
MONTH 18Critical use cases operating; impact simulation and PR-time lineage diff on Tier-1; column-level depth where incidents demanded it; expansion priced from measured cost per use case.

Deliberately never on any team's list: "map everything" lineage crawls · estate-wide enforcement mandates · L4 everywhere · blocking on anomaly scores · automation before two manual samples. What a program refuses to do is half of its vision.
